# Recommendation and product research

Read on 4 November 2026 across **89 distinct website domains**: 23 recommendation sources, 22 interface sources, 22 community/product sources and 22 integration/quality sources. Each group exceeds the requested minimum of 15. These are design inputs, not evidence that DailyCollabs increases retention or causes addiction.

The implemented baseline combines explicit follows, saved content, joined communities and bounded recent feedback with recency and source diversity. Users can inspect interests, tune ranking, hide or mute content, switch modes and reset learned history. Explicit searches retain their topic and language constraints.

The four JSON ledgers record the actual page read, a short finding and its implementation decision. The manifest lists actual domains, including documented replacements for inaccessible planned sources. Some sources describe historical systems or research prototypes; their numeric performance is not transferred to this app.

## Sources and decisions

### agent1

- [Deep Neural Networks for YouTube Recommendations](https://research.google/pubs/deep-neural-networks-for-youtube-recommendations/) — `research.google`. Candidate generation and final ranking serve different objectives and can be maintained as separate stages. **Decision:** Preserve topic and language constraints during retrieval, then personalize only eligible candidates. Do not claim the local weighted ranker is a trained deep model.
- [Adapting the Facebook Reels RecSys AI Model Based on User Feedback](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/01/14/ml-applications/adapting-the-facebook-reels-recsys-ai-model-based-on-user-feedback/) — `fb.com`. Direct interest feedback provides information beyond likes or watch time; sparse feedback and sampling bias still require care. **Decision:** Give explicit saved topics, saves and hides clear influence; provide an editable preference view rather than treating time spent as proof of satisfaction.
- [Engineering the next generation of LinkedIn’s Feed](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed) — `linkedin.com`. Content understanding, evolving interaction history, freshness and production latency jointly shape a useful feed. **Decision:** Keep recent topic signals distinct from longer-lived interests, use bounded history, and retain a simple explainable baseline at this project’s scale.
- [Pinterest Labs — Research & Innovation](https://labs.pinterest.com/) — `pinterest.com`. Pinterest describes content representations that connect pins, boards, products and queries across retrieval and ranking, with responsible AI throughout the lifecycle. **Decision:** Use shared content metadata across communities, bookmarks and search instead of isolated feature-specific profiles. The original pinterestlabs.com URL redirected to this actual domain.
- [Netflix Recommendations: Beyond the 5 stars (Part 1)](https://netflixtechblog.com/netflix-recommendations-beyond-the-5-stars-part-1-55838468f429) — `netflixtechblog.com`. This historical engineering account distinguishes rating accuracy from the whole recommendation experience and emphasizes diversity, freshness and explanations. **Decision:** Show why an item was recommended, diversify the list, and avoid presenting a proxy metric as demonstrated user benefit. These are historical design lessons, not current Netflix weights.
- [How to Use Our Taste Profile Feature to Shape Your Spotify Homepage](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-09-23/taste-profile-shape-home-feed/) — `spotify.com`. Spotify exposes a readable interpretation of taste and permits users to steer or remove preference input. **Decision:** Expose interest sources and a reset/pause or chronological control so a one-off interaction does not permanently define the feed.
- [The history of Amazon’s recommendation algorithm](https://www.amazon.science/the-history-of-amazons-recommendation-algorithm) — `amazon.science`. Item relationships can be a practical recommendation signal; chronological evaluation better reflects predicting subsequent preferences than reconstructing old consumption. **Decision:** Bootstrap from metadata of genuinely saved items and selected communities, and evaluate ordering on time-separated fixtures before considering a learned model.
- [Overview of search result ranking in SharePoint Server](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/search/overview-of-search-result-ranking) — `microsoft.com`. Ranking combines query-dependent and query-independent features, with cheaper early stages and more selective later work. **Decision:** Keep query relevance separate from freshness and account affinity, and calculate expensive enrichment only for the candidates actually returned.
- [SEMORec: A Scalarized Efficient Multi-Objective Recommendation Framework](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/semorec) — `apple.com`. Multiple recommendation objectives require an explicit tradeoff, and the ability to tune objective importance can matter operationally. **Decision:** Keep relevance, recency and diversity weights named and bounded; retain deterministic tests and user controls instead of an opaque single engagement score.
- [How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you?lang=en) — `tiktok.com`. The published explanation separates strong and weak feedback, cold-start interests, negative controls and deliberate interruption of repetitive patterns. **Decision:** Offer not-interested feedback, deduplicate results, and avoid repeated same-source runs while keeping explicit topic search relevant.
- [For You Home Timeline Recommendations](https://help.x.com/en/resources/recommender-systems/for-you-home-timeline-recommendations) — `x.com`. X describes sourcing recent relevant candidates, interaction-based ranking, filtering and a reverse-chronological Following alternative. **Decision:** Separate personalized For you from a chronological Latest mode and ensure users can hide irrelevant recommendations.
- [X’s Recommendation Algorithm — repository architecture](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm) — `github.com`. The published repository documents distinct user-signal, candidate-source, ranking and post-mixing/filtering components. **Decision:** Keep event capture, interest-profile assembly and pure list ranking in separate local modules; use architectural lessons without copying repository implementation code.
- [Wide & Deep Learning for Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07792) — `arxiv.org`. The paper’s complete abstract explains the tradeoff between memorizing known feature interactions and generalizing to unseen combinations, including sparse-data risks. **Decision:** Use content metadata for cold start and bounded account evidence for affinity. Defer learned embeddings until sufficient legitimate training data exists.
- [ACM RecSys 2026 — Call for Contributions](https://recsys.acm.org/recsys26/call/) — `acm.org`. The conference’s evaluation guidance values reproducibility, user control, diversity and metrics beyond accuracy, with evidence aligned to claims. **Decision:** Document ranking inputs and limitations, add reproducible tests for intended behavior, and avoid claiming increased retention from source review or unit tests.
- [SciPy cosine distance reference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.distance.cosine.html) — `scipy.org`. Cosine distance is one minus normalized dot-product similarity; confusing distance with similarity reverses ranking intent. **Decision:** Use normalized, bounded affinity and explicit zero-evidence behavior in ranking tests. This successfully read source replaces the IEEE bot-verification shell.
- [Stanford CS246 — Recommender Systems I](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs246/slides/07-recsys1.pdf) — `stanford.edu`. The course distinguishes content-based and collaborative methods and identifies cold start, sparsity, first-rater and popularity-bias limitations. **Decision:** Start with content-based topic overlap plus explicit user choices; reserve some source variety instead of assuming popularity is universal relevance.
- [Optimizing Search Engines using Clickthrough Data](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~tj/publications/joachims_02c.pdf) — `cornell.edu`. Clicks depend on the displayed ranking as well as relevance; an unclicked item may simply have been unseen, so a click is not an absolute relevance judgment. **Decision:** Do not interpret all unclicked stories as dislikes. Weight explicit hides and saves distinctly, and use impression-aware evaluation before learning from click logs.
- [Technique protects privacy when making online recommendations](https://news.mit.edu/2022/privacy-online-recommendations-0512) — `mit.edu`. MIT describes privacy risks in feature-vector recommendation and research separating useful nearest-neighbor output from unnecessary disclosure. **Decision:** Keep each interest profile private to its account, bound retained event metadata, and do not expose other users’ histories. The project does not claim to implement the paper’s cryptographic protocol.
- [RecSys Challenge 2024 — News recommendation task and evaluation](https://www.recsyschallenge.com/2024/) — `recsyschallenge.com`. News recommendation combines rapidly decaying items and implicit interests; evaluation includes nDCG/MRR/AUC and diversity, novelty, serendipity and coverage. **Decision:** Apply recency decay to news, deduplicate repeated stories and test source coverage alongside relevance; do not optimize only clicks.
- [A Survey of Accuracy Evaluation Metrics of Recommendation Tasks](https://jmlr.org/papers/v10/gunawardana09a.html) — `jmlr.org`. The complete published abstract explains that evaluation metrics must match the task; an inappropriate metric can select the wrong algorithm. **Decision:** Use ordering and coverage checks appropriate to a news feed, and label offline results separately from real user outcomes. This source replaces unreadable RecBole pages.
- [TensorFlow Recommenders — Recommending movies: retrieval](https://www.tensorflow.org/recommenders/examples/basic_retrieval) — `tensorflow.org`. The tutorial separates efficient candidate retrieval from fine ranking and illustrates training/test preparation and implicit versus explicit evidence. **Decision:** Keep the current retrieval/ranking boundary explicit and measure a simple baseline before adding training infrastructure; absence of interaction is not a product-level dislike signal.
- [Introduction to TorchRec](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/intermediate/torchrec_intro_tutorial.html) — `pytorch.org`. Embedding representations are trained from labeled examples; large-scale recommendation tables require deliberate memory and infrastructure planning. **Decision:** Do not label random or untrained vectors as intelligence. Use real account metadata and transparent rules now, and keep a future learned-model interface separate.
- [scikit-learn — ndcg_score](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.ndcg_score.html) — `scikit-learn.org`. nDCG discounts lower-ranked items and normalizes against the ideal ordering; cutoff and tie handling affect the measured result. **Decision:** When graded relevance labels exist, evaluate top-k ordering with a documented cutoff and tie policy; retain deterministic order for equal scores.

### agent2

- [10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — `nngroup.com`. Visible status, familiar language, recovery, and user control help people understand outcomes and choose their next step. **Decision:** Show the active feed mode and confirm explicit feedback; keep reset and recovery actions easy to find.
- [Display Applied Filters in an Overview](https://baymard.com/research-articles/how-to-design-applied-filters) — `baymard.com`. Applied-filter summaries give immediate confirmation and make it easier to broaden results without reopening hidden filter panels. These observations come from ecommerce and are adapted to content discovery. **Decision:** Keep selected topics and languages visible near the feed and provide direct removal or reset controls.
- [Hick’s Law](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/) — `lawsofux.com`. More numerous or complicated choices can slow decisions; progressive disclosure can reduce initial cognitive load. **Decision:** Keep a small primary set of feed controls and place detailed preference tuning behind a clearly labeled control.
- [Priority Guides: A Content-First Alternative to Wireframes](https://alistapart.com/article/priority-guides-a-content-first-alternative-to-wireframes/) — `alistapart.com`. Prioritize real content by user goals before arranging the layout, starting with the most important mobile information. **Decision:** Put the real product display, concrete product value, and account action ahead of decorative marketing; prioritize useful content rather than time spent.
- [Infinite Scrolling, Pagination Or Load More Buttons? Usability Findings In eCommerce](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/03/pagination-infinite-scrolling-load-more-buttons/) — `smashingmagazine.com`. The authors distinguish exploratory browsing from ranked search and identify footer access and return-position problems with automatic infinite loading. This is not direct evidence about a news feed. **Decision:** For ranked topic results, prefer an explicit batch/end state and preserve the reader’s position; treat a manual Load more option as a recommended refinement rather than optimizing endless scrolling.
- [Context is the new interface](https://uxdesign.cc/context-is-the-new-interface-c16b7f6e77be) — `uxdesign.cc`. Preferences and past behavior should inform continuity without becoming unquestioned assumptions; people need to inspect and correct the context used. **Decision:** Explain which explicit interests influence the feed and allow clearing learned preferences without changing the person’s account.
- [Learned Helplessness in AI-Assisted Work: When Users Stop Trying to Shape the Tools They Use](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2026/04/learned-helplessness-in-ai-assisted-work-when-users-stop-trying-to-shape-the-tools-they-use.php) — `uxmatters.com`. Corrections need visible consequences and acknowledgements so users can see a connection between their input and system behavior. **Decision:** When Show less is selected, confirm the change and actually reduce the relevant signal; do not display success if persistence fails.
- [Talk to Me! Feedback and Notifications in Mobile Design](https://assets.interaction-design.org/literature/article/talk-to-me-feedback-and-notifications-in-mobile-design) — `interaction-design.org`. Feedback should fit the person’s goal, arrive promptly, and avoid unnecessary interruption or arbitrary gamification. **Decision:** Use one concise status update after a save, share, or relevance adjustment; avoid repeated engagement prompts, artificial streaks, and unrelated badges.
- [Search — Material Design](https://m1.material.io/patterns/search.html) — `material.io`. Search helps people locate content in a large information space; predictable access and suitable search patterns improve discovery. This is stable legacy guidance, not a claim about a new 2026 trend. **Decision:** Keep topic search discoverable, retain the active query, and distinguish search results from a general personalized feed.
- [Accessibility — Atlassian Design](https://atlassian.design/foundations/accessibility/) — `atlassian.design`. Consistency, plain language, personal control, text alternatives, and respecting motion preferences are part of end-to-end accessible experiences. **Decision:** Label personalization actions in plain language, include text with state icons, and preserve account and OS motion preferences.
- [Focus management — Primer](https://primer.style/accessibility/design-guidance/focus-management/) — `primer.style`. Keyboard focus must remain visible, follow a logical order, enter new dialogs, and return to a sensible place after they close. **Decision:** Relevance menus and reset dialogs should be keyboard-operable and return focus to their trigger; card drag actions must retain a standard button alternative.
- [Content — Carbon Design System](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/content) — `carbondesignsystem.com`. Clear, purposeful interface language and standardized terminology make navigation and product behavior more coherent. **Decision:** Use one label per outcome across the app: Saved, Shared, Show less, Latest, and For you; avoid interchangeable terms that imply different ranking behavior.
- [App Design Guidelines — Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design) — `shopify.dev`. Matching the appearance and behaviors of the surrounding product reduces relearning and supports trust across workflows. **Decision:** Make feed controls and recommendation feedback use the same buttons, spacing, and input behavior as communities and settings.
- [Writing for errors — Adobe Spectrum](https://spectrum.adobe.com/content/writing-for-errors) — `adobe.com`. Useful error messages explain what happened and provide a concrete path forward; the component should fit the consequence of the failure. **Decision:** Keep failed feed loads and failed preference saves contextual, preserve the reader’s current state, and provide Try again instead of silently resetting the feed.
- [Design principles — Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/design-principles) — `microsoft.design`. Familiar platform behavior, less visual clutter, and inclusion help people stay focused on their own task. **Decision:** Give article/video content more visual weight than recommendation controls, while keeping those controls discoverable and consistent on mobile.
- [Notification banner — GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/notification-banner/) — `service.gov.uk`. A notification banner is for relevant service-wide or cross-page information; not every contextual message belongs in a prominent banner. **Decision:** Confirm per-card feedback near the card or in a concise status region; reserve a service banner for a feed-wide outage or important cross-page change.
- [Checklist — The A11Y Project](https://www.a11yproject.com/checklist/) — `a11yproject.com`. Plain content, keyboard access, semantics, labels, media alternatives, and contrast need to be checked together; a checklist alone does not guarantee accessibility. **Decision:** Review new feed controls with keyboard-only navigation and visible focus, and keep every icon action named and usable without hover.
- [Design for understanding — Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/design/) — `digital.gov`. Logical structure, descriptive headings, scanning aids, and restrained emphasis make content easier to understand. **Decision:** Use descriptive feed headings and readable article metadata; make the current mode understandable without a paragraph of algorithm jargon.
- [Accessibility — Designsystemet](https://designsystemet.no/en/intro/accessibility) — `designsystemet.no`. Accessible base components still rely on correct semantic HTML, focus management, language metadata, token use, and meaningful content. **Decision:** Use native controls and the app’s semantic tokens for feed preferences; treat language selection as meaningful content filtering and preserve accessible labels.
- [Design Systems Gallery — Design Systems Repo](https://designsystemsrepo.com/design-systems/) — `designsystemsrepo.com`. The curated product gallery exposes established design systems whose patterns connect reusable components with real product implementations. It is a reference directory, not an independent outcome study. **Decision:** Use documented product patterns and real app components as references; show the actual implemented interface in the landing preview rather than an invented mock product.
- [Design Systems Report 2026](https://report.zeroheight.com/2026) — `zeroheight.com`. The practitioner survey distinguishes component adoption measures from outcome measures and reports continuing gaps in accessibility, patterns, and documentation. **Decision:** Evaluate the feed by successful discovery, saves, correction usefulness, and low error rates; do not treat longer sessions or more scrolling alone as proof of value.
- [Building Accessibility Into a Canvas-Based Product](https://www.figma.com/blog/building-accessibility-into-a-canvas-based-product/) — `figma.com`. Figma’s engineering account shows that a visually rich interaction must also expose synchronized focus, semantics, and announcements for keyboard and screen-reader users. **Decision:** Keep personalization and card actions represented in semantic DOM controls with announced outcomes, even where the visible interaction also supports dragging or motion.

### agent3

- [Feeds](https://docs.daily.dev/feeds/) — `daily.dev`. Personalized, Following, and date-oriented feeds serve different reading intentions. **Decision:** Expose Balanced, Fresh, Discover, and Following choices instead of one opaque ranking.
- [Community Onboarding FAQ](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/11074987197975-Community-Onboarding-FAQ) — `discord.com`. Members can choose relevant channels and roles and revise those choices later. **Decision:** Treat joined communities and explicitly followed topics as editable interests.
- [Reddit’s Approach to Content Recommendations](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/23511859482388-Reddit-s-Approach-to-Content-Recommendations) — `reddithelp.com`. Recommendations combine content, activity, subscriptions, settings, filtering, explanations, and negative feedback. **Decision:** Provide personalization off, muted topics, truthful story reasons, and explicit more/less feedback.
- [Understanding Discourse Trust Levels](https://blog.discourse.org/2018/06/understanding-discourse-trust-levels/) — `discourse.org`. Community participation and permission systems need explicit, understandable rules; newer permissions can target groups. **Decision:** Keep owner/member permissions distinct from feed ranking and avoid fabricated reputation inputs.
- [What are tags, and how should I use them?](https://stackoverflow.com/help/tagging) — `stackoverflow.com`. Clear topic tags connect people to relevant questions and explain what a category means. **Decision:** Use visible followed-topic chips as explicit interest signals rather than hidden broad category assumptions.
- [Help:Watchlist](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Help:Watchlist) — `mediawiki.org`. A personal watchlist narrows a busy update stream to pages the reader chooses and can unwatch. **Decision:** Store followed topics per authenticated account and make removal easy.
- [Reading strategies](https://zulip.com/help/reading-strategies) — `zulip.com`. Conversation, recent, combined, and focused views support different ways of catching up. **Decision:** Offer a Fresh reading mode and keep channel conversation navigation separate from discovery controls.
- [Public Rooms](https://matrix.org/docs/chat_basics/public-rooms/) — `matrix.org`. People discover communities through topic search, directories, previews, and explicit joining. **Decision:** Use real community joins as an interest source and preserve an easy path from discovery into community channels.
- [Get to know Feed](https://help.circle.so/p/basics/home/home-overview) — `circle.so`. Membership shapes the feed, and the For you sort combines recent and active posts. **Decision:** Combine community affinity with recency while keeping freshness adjustable and the cold-start feed usable.
- [Understanding and setting up a Feed](https://bettermode.com/hub/content-management/post/understanding-and-setting-up-a-feed-dG1qPBbU4OiTEOb) — `bettermode.com`. The feed aggregates posts from spaces a member has joined. **Decision:** Label community-derived interests plainly and keep a Following balance option.
- [Community Features](https://www.mightynetworks.com/community) — `mightynetworks.com`. Community spaces combine feeds, chat, questions, events, and member discovery. **Decision:** Connect feed discovery to actual community participation instead of inventing engagement activity.
- [How can I recommend other publications on Substack?](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/5036794583828-How-can-I-recommend-other-publications-on-Substack) — `substack.com`. A short reason can help readers decide whether a recommendation is worth exploring; recommendations can be removed. **Decision:** Show Why this story only when real backend explanations exist, and keep topic choices reversible.
- [Recommendations](https://ghost.org/help/recommendations/) — `ghost.org`. Publishers can recommend other sites with a customized title and description. **Decision:** Retain recognizable source metadata and explain discovery without suggesting an unverified endorsement.
- [WordPress Feeds](https://developer.wordpress.org/advanced-administration/wordpress/feeds/) — `wordpress.org`. RSS and Atom expose recent publisher content, while comment feeds are a distinct stream. **Decision:** Preserve publisher source and publication metadata so freshness ranking reflects actual article data.
- [Refine recommendations](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/224488047-Refine-recommendations) — `medium.com`. Readers can manage follows and mutes, hide stories, and clear reading history to adjust recommendations. **Decision:** Add followed and muted topic controls plus a clearly scoped reset of learned reading feedback.
- [Customizing Your Feed](https://dev.to/help/customizing-your-feed) — `dev.to`. Followed tags/users shape recommendations; hidden tags can be managed and restored; saved reading lists are separate. **Decision:** Keep explicit follows, mutes, and bookmarks distinct from learned history, with editable topic chips.
- [The journey behind Hashnode’s new personalized feed](https://townhall.hashnode.com/revamped-personalized-feed) — `hashnode.com`. Followed tags, authors, subscriptions, and reading interests help make a feed relevant. **Decision:** Use real explicit and behavioral interests and expose their provenance in the Tune your feed dialog.
- [How can I add/create a Mute Filter?](https://docs.feedly.com/article/109-how-can-i-add-create-a-mute-filter) — `feedly.com`. Keyword or phrase filters can remove unwanted content across chosen feeds. **Decision:** Allow up to 20 editable muted topics and persist them on the server.
- [Read only the articles you want](https://www.inoreader.com/features/filters) — `inoreader.com`. Server-side filters keep or remove matching articles consistently across devices; duplicate filtering reduces repeated stories. **Decision:** Apply muted preferences before ranking and preserve source diversity and duplicate suppression in the feed.
- [How to Personalize Flipboard](https://about.flipboard.com/inside-flipboard/how-to-personalize-flipboard/) — `flipboard.com`. Readers can tune followed topics, request less of a story type, mute sources, and inspect why a story appeared. **Decision:** Make Tune your feed accessible from the feed and pair adjustable recommendations with honest reasons.
- [Daily Digest](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/daily-digest) — `readwise.io`. Discovery can resurface saved content alongside new feed items, while the digest remains optional. **Decision:** Keep bookmarks useful after a history reset and avoid forced streaks or attention-pressure badges.
- [Guardian app](https://www.theguardian.com/info/2026/aug/27/discover-a-better-way-to-experience-the-guardian-with-the-guardian-app) — `theguardian.com`. Followed topics personalize news. **Decision:** Keep followed topics explicit and editable.

### integration

- [Understanding Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) — `w3.org`. Moving or automatically updating content needs an effective pause mechanism. **Decision:** Provide an explicit film pause control, visibility suspension and reduced-motion support.
- [Intersection Observer API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Intersection_Observer_API) — `mozilla.org`. The browser can report visibility asynchronously without continuous scroll polling. **Decision:** Suspend the landing film outside the viewport and use lazy media loading.
- [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) — `web.dev`. Loading, responsiveness and visual stability jointly affect the experience. **Decision:** Bound parallel retrieval, reserve media dimensions and lazy-load card imagery; measure rather than claim a performance score.
- [Authorization Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html) — `owasp.org`. Authorization belongs on each resource and action, with least privilege and explicit tests. **Decision:** Derive actors from verified JWTs for preferences, comments, bookmarks and notifications; test cross-account access.
- [Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers) — `ftc.gov`. Deceptive presentation can obstruct informed user choice. **Decision:** Keep preference controls, undo, reset and disclosure visible; do not fabricate urgency or social proof.
- [User Controls: Do YouTube’s Controls Work?](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/research/library/user-controls/) — `mozillafoundation.org`. The study found a gap between expected and actual effects of recommendation controls. **Decision:** Test that mute, hide and explicit feedback really change eligibility or order. ICO returned 403, so this readable original study replaces it.
- [DSA impact on platforms](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-impact-platforms) — `europa.eu`. Transparent explanations and meaningful recommendation choices are emphasized. **Decision:** Explain actual ranking reasons and expose chronological and personalization-off modes. This is a design lesson, not a legal-compliance claim.
- [Evaluating recommender systems](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/safety-technology/evaluating-recommender-systems-in-relation-to-the-dissemination-of-illegal-and-harmful-content-in-the-uk) — `ofcom.org.uk`. Recommendation outcomes require ongoing evaluation with different methods. **Decision:** Verify ranking properties and account isolation now; defer claims about retention or satisfaction until measured with users.
- [Privacy Without Monopoly](https://www.eff.org/wp/interoperability-and-privacy) — `eff.org`. Interoperability requires informed choice, data minimization and clear boundaries around shared information. **Decision:** Keep feed learning first-party and account-scoped; sharing starts from an explicit user action.
- [We should know all our data and profiles](https://www.privacyinternational.org/our-demands/we-should-know-all-our-data-and-profiles) — `privacyinternational.org`. Inferred preferences can reveal information people did not knowingly provide. **Decision:** Show inferred interest provenance, support editing and history reset, and avoid outside tracking.
- [What Is News?](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2025/05/13/what-is-news/) — `pewresearch.org`. Audiences bring different expectations about timeliness, relevance and what qualifies as news. **Decision:** Keep publisher, publication time and content format visible; do not label every video as verified reporting.
- [Constant Companion](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/constant-companion-a-week-in-the-life-of-a-young-persons-smartphone-use) — `commonsensemedia.org`. The youth-focused study describes notification overload and complicated benefits and costs of personalized media. **Decision:** Send only actual comment-interaction events, deduplicate notifications and keep read controls. Do not generalize the youth sample to every user.
- [Local audiences consuming news on social platforms are hungry for transparency](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center_reports/focus-groups-news-media-diet.php) — `cjr.org`. Focus groups reported convenience alongside concern about opaque feed selection and data collection. **Decision:** Pair useful discovery with readable recommendation reasons and visible preference sources.
- [Digital News Report 2026: Overview and key findings](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary) — `ox.ac.uk`. Survey findings describe growing video-led news use alongside concerns about overload and trust. **Decision:** Offer relevant video alongside stories, preserve source links and keep viewing a deliberate action.
- [IFCN Code of Principles: The commitments](https://ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/the-commitments) — `poynter.org`. Traceable evidence and transparent methodology let readers inspect conclusions. **Decision:** Retain original publisher links and distinguish ranking explanations from fact-checking or certification.
- [The 8 Trust Indicators](https://thetrustproject.org/trust-indicators/) — `thetrustproject.org`. Source identity, labels, methods, references, diverse voices and actionable feedback help readers evaluate news. **Decision:** Display publisher and format metadata and offer feedback without inventing a trust badge. Actual domain corrects the planned trustproject.org.
- [Website Rating Process and Criteria](https://www.newsguardtech.com/ratings/rating-process-criteria/) — `newsguardtech.com`. NewsGuard describes an editorial assessment with evidence-backed criteria, separate from user-platform content. **Decision:** Do not manufacture reliability scores from engagement; content reasons describe relevance only.
- [How we fact check](https://fullfact.org/about/how-we-fact-check/) — `fullfact.org`. Full Fact prioritizes public-interest claims, primary evidence and corrections. **Decision:** Keep original links accessible and preserve published metadata; no claim that this aggregator fact-checks every story. Search returned readable first-party article text although direct open returned 410.
- [Journalism Trust Initiative](https://rsf.org/en/journalism-trust-initiative) — `rsf.org`. JTI evaluates journalism processes and transparency rather than determining the truth of each individual item. **Decision:** Keep relevance ranking separate from source certification; do not claim JTI participation.
- [The Trust Principles](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/about-us/trust-principles) — `thomsonreuters.com`. Reuters emphasizes independence, integrity and reliable information serving varied interests. **Decision:** Preserve publisher identity and source variety instead of optimizing only click counts. This readable official page replaces inaccessible reuters.com.
- [News values and principles](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/) — `ap.org`. AP describes standards for accuracy and avoidance of bias and conflicts of interest. **Decision:** Treat source attribution and original context as essential metadata; do not imply AP endorsement.
- [EXPIRE](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/expire/) — `redis.io`. Expiration bounds how long cached data remains available, and update behavior must be understood. **Decision:** Use bounded cache and signal lifetimes with explicit invalidation. Redis is a research reference; this implementation uses bounded process caches and MongoDB TTL, not a Redis service.
